### **CORE NARRATIVE**
Georges Sagnac (1869–1928) conducted his rotating interferometer experiment in 1913, observing a phase shift in counter-propagating light beams. He interpreted this "Sagnac effect" as definitive proof of the luminiferous aether and a direct refutation of Albert Einstein’s Special Theory of Relativity (STR), which posited a constant speed of light in all frames. Mainstream physics universally absorbed the Sagnac effect as fully consistent with relativistic kinematics (rotating/non-inertial frames). However, a persistent lineage of physicists, engineers, and dissident researchers have defended Sagnac’s original interpretation, arguing the effect proves an absolute reference frame and invalidates Einstein.

While the mainstream viewed this as confirming relativity, Michelson and aether-sympathizers viewed it as confirming a stationary aether.
*   **Herbert E. Ives** [AltMedia/Academic]: A prominent Bell Labs physicist in the mid-20th century.
